# Everyday prose, Austened

Six pieces of ordinary modern prose (in [samples/](../samples/)), and what Austen Advisor's engine makes of them **with no language model**: every word Austen never wrote replaced by her nearest word in meaning (WordNet senses, her own inflections, her trigram model and word vectors), contractions written out where she wrote them out, words swapped where her usage strongly prefers another, and runs of short sentences joined as she joined clauses. Every rewritten sentence is listed with a one-sentence reason (generated by the engine from a template: what it can see changed) and the sentence of hers that shows it, cited.

This is a first draft by statistics, and it shows both what statistics catch and what they cannot: a word chosen for its dictionary sense without its idiom (“said countenance” for “said okay”), a modern thing with no Regency word (“phone”, “hoodie”), left alone and listed. That gap is what “Rewrite with AI” is for: Claude rewrites the passage with this same engine as its tools, checks its draft against Austen’s works, and cites the sentences it learned from.
