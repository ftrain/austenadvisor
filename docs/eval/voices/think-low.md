## Faults

### austen / breakup (f3 v3 s4 n4 n3 t3 s4)

_A competent, mostly faithful Austenian rewrite with a good opening period and sound structure. It adds an unreported incident ('did not at first observe me'), leaves the ice cream anachronism jarring, and its notes are sparse with doubtful citations. The lesson is useful but narrow._

- **fidelity**: “so intent upon what he held in his hand that he did not at first observe me”: The writer says only that he was already there, scrolling on his phone. 'Did not at first observe me' is a new incident, and 'intent upon' changes idle scrolling into absorption. The notes do not report either change.
- **fidelity**: “I was not far from tears, but did not cry”: The writer's 'I wanted to cry but I didn't' is a plain wish. 'Not far from tears' shifts it to a physical near-state and softens the degree of feeling a little.
- **voice**: “and ate a whole pint of ice cream”: The modern 'pint of ice cream' sits oddly beside 'coffee-house' and 'any thing', so the period diction slips. The paragraph's sentence also keeps the writer's flat rhythm instead of becoming a balanced Austen period.
- **voice**: “Ryan wrote to me at midnight”: Austen is almost entirely third-person narration, so the first-person memoir narrator is not her natural mode. 'Wrote to me' and 'for that is a sentence which nobody, I believe…' read as Austen in costume more than as her ironic narrator.
- **notes**: “cites Emma, ch. 23, ¶ 28”: The chapter and paragraph citations for Emma and Persuasion look precise but are doubtful and cannot be verified. The sources do not clearly show the claimed move, so they are not apt.
- **notes**: “Paragraph 3:
  (no notes)
Paragraph 4:
  (no notes)”: Real changes go unreported in paragraphs 3 and 4: 'roommate' became 'the friend who shared my room', 'overreacting' became 'made too much of it', and 'wanted to cry' was reworked. The notes also claim 'one liberty' while the added 'did not at first observe me' goes unmentioned.
- **teaching**: “Practise joining short sentences with '; and' and 'for,' and understating the strongest feeling”: The lesson is thin, with essentially one idea (join sentences, understate). It ends with a stray closing quotation mark. It says nothing about the paragraph 4 verdict or about showing a character's conduct before his speech, which the outline names.

### austen / neighbors (f3 v3 s4 n3 n3 t3 s4)

_A competent Austen pastiche that catches the contradiction in the draft and mends it with a reported sister, but it adds invented content (the neighbourhood, the mother's habit), repeats 'however', and has some stiff or redundant phrasing. The teaching is decent but includes a shaky citation and a vague number._

- **fidelity**: “who was known to be his sister”: The sister is invented. The mending is reported, but the draft says "the mom", and the rewrite turns her into a different relation. The mending is defensible, but 'known to be' adds a fact the writer never gave.
- **fidelity**: “so that the whole neighbourhood was at once furnished with matter for observation”: Adds an event: the whole neighbourhood's reaction. The writer's passage has no such thing. The note calls it a liberty, but it is invented content.
- **fidelity**: “a cart of uncommon size”: A 'huge truck' becomes a cart, which is an anachronistic substitution. 'Cart' also loses the sense of a moving truck.
- **fidelity**: “It soon appeared, however, that the father was a lawyer”: 'however' is misplaced and repeated. It already appears in the previous paragraph, and nothing here contrasts with what came before.
- **fidelity**: “now, as if on purpose, a single man, and a lawyer, had come to the very door.”: Adds the narrator's ironic comment and a 'coincidence' that the draft lacks. The writer's wry reluctance ('I knew exactly what she was thinking') is shifted, and the blunt closing verdict is lost.
- **fidelity**: “a dish of biscuits”: 'Cookies' becomes 'biscuits', which is acceptable. But the 'right away' urgency is rendered as a general habit ('never suffered a new neighbour to remain a day'), a character trait the writer did not give.
- **voice**: “had a look which I understood at once, for I knew exactly what was passing in her mind”: Redundant: 'understood at once' and 'knew exactly' say the same thing. The sentence is clumsy and un-Austenian in its repetition.
- **voice**: “to see me married”: Fine, but the draft's narrator is an unspecified gender and age. Austen's heroine voice or Mrs. Bennet-like mother ironies are borrowed rather than built. The prose lacks Austen's free indirect discourse.
- **natural**: “of their going to church”: Awkward: 'their going to church' reads stiffly and loses the sense of whether they were churchgoers.
- **natural**: “on the Saturday last”: Odd idiom for 'last weekend'. It is not period-natural and narrows 'weekend' to one day.
- **notes**: “as Bingley's unmarried sisters live with him in Pride and Prejudice”: The comparison is loosely apt but the sisters are in the household for different reasons. The notes also claim a quotation 'cites Sense and Sensibility, ch. 6, ¶ 6' for a sentence that is the rewriter's own, which is a dubious citation.
- **notes**: “the mother's scheme is left to land last, where "as if on purpose" says more than it says”: The 'says more than it says' phrase is a muddled formulation, and the lesson talks of 'near twice a dozen' words, which is vague and rhetorical, not concrete.
- **sense**: “or, more properly, that he had been married, and was now parted from his wife”: Mostly mends 'single, well, divorced', but 'single man' then contradicts 'married' wording: the correction is the draft's own and fine, though 'single' in the last sentence glosses over it.

### kjv / breakup (f3 v4 s4 n3 n2 t3 s4)

_A broadly faithful, recognisably Biblical rewrite that keeps the story's order and feeling, but with a few slips ('pot', 'weighty thing', 'speak together'), some strained phrasing, and notes that are thin, partly inaccurate, and missing for the last two paragraphs. The lesson is useful in places but contradicts itself on 'feeling by its sign'._

- **fidelity**: “and he said, The fault is not in thee, but it is in me.”: The writer's 'it wasn't me, it was him' is kept, but 'I am not ready for a weighty thing' softens 'something serious' into an odd, vague abstraction; the breakup sense is blurred.
- **fidelity**: “Ryan sent word unto me, saying, We must speak together”: The writer's 'texted' and 'He said we needed to talk' are conflated, and 'I knew right away what that meant' is kept. 'Sent word' loses the text-message medium, though that is acceptable; the 'He said' is merged into 'saying'.
- **fidelity**: “his eyes were upon his phone; and his countenance was troubled”: 'Scrolling' is lost, and 'He looked nervous' becomes 'troubled', which shifts the feeling from nervous to distressed.
- **fidelity**: “a whole pot of ice cream”: The writer wrote 'pint'; 'pot' changes the measure and sounds odd, not Biblical.
- **fidelity**: “And I would have wept, but I wept not”: 'I wanted to cry but I didn't' is rendered as 'would have wept'; this is an acceptable shift, but 'I wept not' sits awkwardly after 'would have wept' and is a slight loss of 'wanted'.
- **natural**: “made more of the matter than was meet”: 'Overreacting' is rendered in a stilted, near-Elizabethan phrase; 'meet' is used in the KJV, but the whole line feels forced. 'The friend that dwelt with me' is acceptable, though 'roommate' is dropped to a vaguer 'friend'.
- **natural**: “and he was there before me”: The writer says only that he was already there. 'Before me' is idiomatic, but the 'and... and... and' chain in this sentence becomes monotonous.
- **natural**: “then I went home in the rain, and did eat”: 'Then' with colon-spliced clauses breaks the otherwise consistent 'And' pattern; 'did eat' is emphatic-do usage not used in KJV narrative like this.
- **voice**: “We must speak together”: 'Speak together' is slightly unnatural; the KJV would say 'commune' or 'we must talk' in some such form, and 'together' is an invented idiom.
- **notes**: “cites 2 Samuel 7:4”: The citation is for 'and it came to pass that night', which is plausible but is only loosely applicable and unverified; it misses the more usual 'And it came to pass at midnight' (Exodus 12:29) precisely.
- **notes**: “Unto, thereof, wherefore: “Ryan sent word unto me” — cites Ezekiel 6:1”: The label lists 'thereof, wherefore' but neither appears in the rewrite; the note is inaccurate about what changed. The citation is also generic.
- **notes**: “(no notes)”: Paragraphs 3 and 4 have no notes, though they contain the most distinctive changes ('I would have wept, but I wept not', 'meet', 'grieved me still'); the lesson refers to the first of these but the notes do not.
- **notes**: “Clauses laid side by side with “and”: “and he was there before me, and his eyes were upon his phone” — cites Zechariah 1:18”: The cited verse ('Then lifted I up mine eyes') is not a clear example of parataxis with 'and'; the citation is weakly matched.
- **teaching**: “which carries the withheld grief more plainly than the denial alone”: The lesson's claim is mostly assertion; 'I would have wept, but I wept not' is hardly a bodily sign but a restated denial, so the 'feeling by its sign' lesson contradicts the example.
- **structure**: “And I would have wept, but I wept not: and I inclined my head”: The writer's short paragraph rhythm is kept, but the rewrite uses colons throughout, which flattens the rhythm; the ending ('but it grieved me still') is kept but the verdict is not shaped to the voice's close.
