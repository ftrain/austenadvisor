### austen / breakup (f3 v3 s3 n2 n2 t3 s4)

_The rewrite keeps the order and most of the facts and has some pleasant Austenian touches (litotes, the aside on the formula), but it carries a visible punctuation glitch, unflagged invented detail about Ryan's scrolling and resolving, and an uneven voice that leaves "okay" and the phone untouched. The notes cite a dubious source, misquote the rewrite in the lesson, and skip real changes._

- **natural**: “I knew immediately what they meant, .”: A stray comma and space before the full stop, evidently a deleted phrase; it reads as a typo and is sloppy.
- **fidelity**: “though his words were brief”: Adds a characterization (brevity) that the writer never gave. The notes admit it, but it is still an addition beyond voice commentary.
- **fidelity**: “as is the custom in such cases, before he could resolve upon the manner of it”: Invents Ryan's inner state, that he was scrolling while resolving how to break things off, and a general custom of scrolling. The writer only said he was scrolling and nervous. This is not noted as a liberty.
- **fidelity**: “having at length begun”: Implies a delay or hesitation before he spoke that is not in the original.
- **voice**: “scrolling on his phone”: The modern anachronism is left plain beside the Regency diction, so the voice reads as a costume; "okay" and "ice-cream" are similarly unconverted.
- **voice**: “said, okay,”: A bare modern "okay" in the middle of Austenian periods jars; Austen would use "very well" or report it indirectly.
- **natural**: “My companion in the house”: A clumsy periphrasis for "roommate" that is not idiomatic Austen; "my friend who shared the house" would be nearer.
- **natural**: “I wished to cry, but I did not; I only nodded, and said, okay, and walked home in the rain and ate a whole pint of ice-cream.”: Flat, unbalanced, with a string of "and"s. The closing bathos of the ice cream gets no Austenian irony or comment, so the paragraph is essentially the original with semicolons.
- **notes**: “cites Emma, ch. 21, ¶ 12”: A precise chapter-and-paragraph citation for the phrase "not a little discomposed" is unverifiable and probably invented; Emma ch. 21 is not a known source for this phrase.
- **notes**: “Slightly formal emphasis on the persistence of the hurt; the writer's "But it still hurt" carries the same force, so no fault, noted only.”: Muddled and self-cancelling: it names a liberty that is not one, and it does not describe the actual change ("for all that", the dash).
- **notes**: “Paragraph 3:
  (no notes)”: Paragraph 3 has several changes (semicolons, "I wished to cry", the lost "Then") that go unreported, and paragraph 2's addition about scrolling is not flagged.
- **teaching**: “what people everywhere say who mean to end an attachment”: The lesson quotes a phrase different from the rewrite's actual "what people say who mean to end an attachment", so the lesson misdescribes the text. It also says the three opening lines become one sentence, but the rewrite's first sentence has the stray comma and is not a balanced period.
- **fidelity**: “My companion in the house thought I made more of the matter than it deserved”: Slightly softens "overreacting", and the dash "but it hurt me for all that" drops the "still", the persistence of the hurt that the writer stressed.
